## 2. The four details

### 2.1 ⛔ AC 3 is still unmet, and the reason is unchanged

The criterion reads: *"enforced and **now actually exercised** — it had been guarding rows nothing
inserted while the writer sat in MVP-2."*

⚠ **The writer returned to MVP-1 on 11 Aug 2026** — `CoilCompletionService` came back with DB7
and DB7b, *"because the coil genealogy behind the welding-wire customer certificates is an MVP-1
obligation"*. **The word "now" in AC 3 refers to that return.**

⛔ **But [`FW-185`](../../Backend/tasks/FW-185.md) is still
unbuilt**, so nothing inserts `CoilTraceability` rows and **the trigger has still never fired.**

**So AC 3 is a *demonstration* owed, not a build** — and it closes when `FW-185` lands, not here.
`P-191`'s rule again: **a guard closes on being exercised, never on existing.**

### 2.2 This story is the evidence for a rule the repository now follows

The `CoilOutput`/`CoilTraceability` history is the clearest case in the project of a scoping
mistake and its correction:

| | |
|---|---|
| The tables were **MVP-1** | while their only writer, `CoilCompletionService`, was **MVP-2** |
| Result | **the DM010 non-overlap trigger guarded rows nothing inserted** |
| Correction | **Phase 9 came back *whole*** on 11 Aug 2026, screens included |
| The rule | ⛔ *"screen deferred therefore tables deferred"* **is not the rule** — and the converse fails too |

⚠ **Worth restating because the same shape recurs**: `FW-171`'s five event tables have their write
paths in five Backend stories, and `FW-159`'s `RodStaging` has its writes in `FW-157`/`FW-158`. **A
table without its writer is a guard nobody tests.**

### 2.3 ⛔ `SkidId` has no parent, and AC 6 asks only that it be said

AC 6: *"`CoilOutput.SkidId` references an external skid table **with no DB FK** — documented as
such."*

⚠ **The criterion is honest**: it does not ask for the FK, because the target does not exist
(`OI-104`). **Documenting it is the deliverable.**

⛔ **Two consequences reach other stories:**
- [`FW-185`](../../Backend/tasks/FW-185.md)'s **2-per-skid
  rule** is a **count**, not a constraint (`P-242`).
- [`FW-187`](../../Backend/tasks/FW-187.md)'s **"skid closed"**
  broadcast has no state to announce (`P-248`).

⚠ **And `G49` B1 adds that there is no slot / position-on-skid column either**, so *1-of-2 versus
2-of-2* is unrecordable. **`OI-104` and B1 together are why the skid is barely modelled at all.**

### 2.4 The trigger is the weaker of two guards

The user story — *"a certificate cannot attribute the same foot to two rods"* — is served by
**two** mechanisms, and the DB one is the weaker:

| Guard | Where | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| `trg_CoilTraceability_NoOverlap` | `CoilTraceability` | ✅ Real — **but never exercised** (§2.1) |
| **`SpoolSegmentsMustNotOverlapRule`** | `FlatWire.Domain/Rules/TraceabilityRules.cs`, invoked at `SpoolProcessing.cs:188` | ✅ Built and invoked — **and on `SpoolTraceability` it is the ONLY defence** |

⛔ **`G42`'s reason matters here**: `SpoolTraceability`'s footage bounds are **nullable**, because
the genealogy is **weight-primary**. **A trigger joining on `NULL` passes silently**, which is why
22 Aug deliberately added none there and why `[TRP §8]`'s *"1 trigger"* still holds.

⚠ **So do not assume the coil-level trigger generalises.** The two levels have different
nullability and therefore different defences.

### `P-252` — AC 3 closes on an observed firing, and it belongs to `FW-185`'s build

§2.1, applying `P-191`. The trigger's existence was never in doubt; **being exercised** is the
criterion, and only the writer can satisfy it.

⛔ **Closing this story without it would restore exactly the state AC 3 was written to end** — a
guard believed effective because it is present.

### `P-253` — the coil-level trigger does not generalise to the spool level

§2.4. `SpoolTraceability`'s bounds are nullable and `CoilTraceability`'s guard assumes they are
not. ⚠ **A future "add the same trigger at spool level" would pass silently and look like
defence-in-depth** — `G42` records why 22 Aug deliberately did not.
