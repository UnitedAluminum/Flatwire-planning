## 2. The four details

### 2.1 ⛔ The story may correctly close at 0 h

AC 6 is explicit: *"if the client says no, the event stays handler-less **by decision**, `P-137`
is restated as settled, and this story closes at 0 h."*

⚠ **That is a real outcome, not a failure**, and it is why AC 1 puts `OI-140` **first**. The
justification is in the card's own note: **the screens do not go dark**. DB7 completes the coil
through request/response, so the operator who acted sees the result immediately.

**What is actually at stake** is whether a *second* operator, on another client on the same line,
should see a coil complete without refreshing. **That is a workflow question**, not a technical
one.

### 2.2 ⛔ Breaking, and therefore one pass

`[API §8]` makes adding a hub member a **breaking change**. `P-22` minted `IFlatWireClient`
**whole** for this reason — *"a published contract from `FW-080`'s first commit"*.

**So all five artifacts move together:**

| Artifact | Change |
|---|---|
| `[SIG §5.2]` | The event |
| `IFlatWireClient` | The member |
| `Models/RealTime/HubContracts.cs` | The payload |
| `[SIG §5.6]` | The Angular mirror |
| `FW-136` | The client leg — ⛔ **which does not exist** |

⚠ **`P-116`'s consequence lands here**: `FW-149` closed the **server** leg at 20 of 20 and its
AC 3 *"still cannot close"* because the client leg is owed to `FW-136`. **This story inherits
that** — its FE 4 h **lands with `FW-136`**, whenever that is.

### 2.3 Every payload field needs a persisted source — and `[SIG §5.5]` is the warning

AC 5 names *"at least `lineId`, `runId`, coil alpha, weight and skid position"*.

⛔ **Check each against the schema before publishing**, because `[API §8]` makes a later addition
breaking **and a later removal worse**:

| Field | Source |
|---|---|
| `lineId`, `runId` | ✅ On the event and the run |
| coil alpha | ✅ `CoilOutput` |
| **weight** | ⛔ **`Q10`** — `LbPerFtFactor` is `NULL`, so the weight may be **unknown** (`P-244`) |
| **skid position** | ⛔ **`G49` B1 — there is NO slot / position-on-skid column.** `FW-244` supplies it |

⚠ **Two of the five named fields have no source today.** This is exactly `FW-149`'s finding on
`SpoolCompletionPromptDue` — **4 of 6 fillable, `targetLb` sourceless** — and
[`FW-227`](FW-227.md)'s `P-205` made it a rule. **Apply it here.**

### 2.4 `FW-187` broadcasts at the same moment, and the two must not overlap

[`FW-187`](FW-187.md) sends `LineStatus` → IDLE and a packing-queue update
**on completion**. This story sends `CoilCompleted`.

⚠ **Three broadcasts, one moment.** They are genuinely different facts — the line went idle, the
packing queue changed, a coil was completed — but a client receiving all three must not render
three separate notifications for one event.

⛔ **And `FW-187`'s skid-closed update depends on `P-242`'s derived skid**, which is not an
operator action. **Settle what "closed" means across both stories** before either publishes a
payload.

## 4. Decisions this plan makes

> `P-##` is continuous across the repository; `P-01`–`P-244` precede this story.

### `P-245` — a field with no persisted source is not published, even if it is named on the card

§2.3, applying `P-205`. Two of AC 5's five named fields have no source: **weight** (`Q10`) and
**skid position** (`G49` B1). ⛔ **Publishing a nullable placeholder is what `SpoolCompletionPromptDue`
did**, and `FW-149` had to record the hole retrospectively on a **published** contract.

**Fallback:** publish the members that have sources, and add the others **when their sources
land** — accepting a second breaking change knowingly, rather than shipping an unfillable member.
⚠ **`FW-244` supplies the slot column**, so sequencing it first removes half the problem.

### `P-246` — the completion moment is designed across `FW-235` and `FW-187` together

§2.4. Three broadcasts fire on one action, and neither story alone can see that. ⛔ **And "skid
closed" is a derived count, not an operator act** (`P-242`) — so the two stories must agree what
they are announcing.
