## 2. The four details

### 2.1 The decision is the gate, and "no" is a real outcome

AC 6 closes the story **at 0 h** if `[SVC §3.4]` says no, with `P-14`/`P-24` restated as settled.
That is not a formality — there is a genuine argument on each side:

| For adding | Against |
|---|---|
| All three are **mutated after insert**, which is the condition a token exists for | The mutations may be **single-actor**: one supervisor dispositions one rejection |
| Three of seven roots is an inconsistency a reader will trip on | ⚠ `ROWVERSION` on a hot table costs write throughput and index width |
| `FW-146`'s `409` arm already exists and guards nothing here | The `409` is only reachable if two actors really can collide |

⚠ **The deciding question is not "is it mutated" but "can two actors mutate it concurrently".**
`WipRejection`'s disposition is the strongest case — `FW-175`'s durable supervisor queue means a
rejection can sit pending while more than one supervisor is connected.

### 2.2 `ALTER`, not a column in `CREATE TABLE`

The tables are deployed, so the columns arrive by `ALTER` — which triggers `P-70`'s rule
**against this story's own output**: the next person building an entity from `CREATE TABLE`
will not see them.

**So the DDL change is not the whole deliverable.** `Schema/FlatWireSchema_*.md` must record
them, and the `ALTER` must be guarded (`IF NOT EXISTS … COL_LENGTH`) so `RunAll` stays
idempotent.

⚠ **`FW-244` and `FW-246` edit the same two files in the same sprint.** Sequence them.

### 2.3 A green mapping is not a working guard

Mapping `IsRowVersion` makes the build green whether or not the column exists — EF will simply
fail at runtime, and only on a concurrent write, which no ordinary walkthrough performs.

⛔ **So AC 4 is the acceptance test, not AC 3.** Two concurrent updates on each of the three,
each returning **`409` through `FW-146`'s arm**. Anything less proves the mapping compiles, not
that the guard works.

### 2.4 This is planned from Backend, and the hours are mostly DB

DB 4 · BE 2, yet the plan lives in `Backend/tasks/`. That is deliberate: **`D-30`
is a domain-model decision** — whether these three aggregate roots need optimistic concurrency —
**with a DB tail**. `[SVC §3.4]` is a backend document, `P-14`/`P-24`/`P-69` are backend
decisions, and the DB half is three guarded `ALTER`s.

⚠ **The DB Orchestration §1.3 points here rather than holding a second plan.** One story, one
home.

## 4. Decisions this plan makes

> `P-##` is continuous across the repository; `P-01`–`P-173` precede this story.

### `P-174` — the decision turns on concurrent mutability, not on mutability

§2.1. *"Mutated after insert"* is established and is **not** in dispute; it is not sufficient
either. A token earns its cost only where two actors can collide, and `WipRejection` is the
strongest case because `FW-175`'s durable queue lets a disposition wait while several
supervisors are connected.

**Recorded because the card's framing invites a yes on the weaker argument**, and a yes for the
wrong reason is hard to revisit once the columns are on disk.

### `P-175` — AC 4 is the acceptance test; AC 3 is not

§2.3. A green `IsRowVersion` mapping proves nothing about a column that may not exist. **Two
concurrent writers, three times, each returning `409`.**
