## 1. Overview

**Business purpose.** Getting material onto a line, correctly, and telling the machine what to do
with it. Rod is staged at a payoff bay and inspected; at check-in the operator confirms the rod,
the payoff position and the die sizes, **acknowledges the pass schedule — and that acknowledgement
is what pushes the PLC tags**. A run opens. Rod-to-rod induction welds are captured here too,
because they happen at the payoff.

⛔ **This is the gate for everything downstream.** No run exists until a check-in succeeds, and the
check-in is also the module's **hardest transaction**: it spans `FlatWireDB` and the shared
`reqsum` / `wip_coil_orders` schema in one `SqlTransaction`, and it writes to a PLC that cannot be
rolled back.

**Functional scope.** Owned by
[`phase-04-rod-checkin-plc-config.md`](../../60-delivery/phases/phase-04-rod-checkin-plc-config.md),
cited not restated. Fifteen activities: Dashboard 2's six-step check-in wizard (FL1/FL3) and
Dashboard 2A's pre-check-in station; `POST /checkin/rod` and `CheckInService`; the payoff-staging
controller; `RodStaging`, the check-in write path and the `INFLAT` write; `PayoffStateChanged` and
the check-in broadcasts; the traveler field list and no-match path; `ITInhibit` conditions 1–2 and
the re-point at the reason lookup; two endpoint hosts; the service identity for unattended PLC
writes; the DB2A line toggle; weld capture; and moving three cross-database writes into check-in.

⚠ **Weld capture is here, not in [`FS-09`](FS-09-in-run-production-events.md), and `FW-063`'s
`phase: 6` is the stale side.** `[REQ §5.6]` is titled *"Weld Event — captured at the Pre-Check-In
station"*; DB4 was retired 1 Aug 2026 and all of `FR-160`–`175` now lands in **Dashboard 2A's
"Mark as welded" dialog**. `[TB §11]` still routes the range to Phase 6.

⚠ **`INFLAT` is a `FlatWireDB`-local status value** and never enters the shared vocabulary (`D-32`).

**Out of scope.**

- **The pass schedule itself** — [`FS-05`](FS-05-pass-schedule-contract.md). This category
  *acknowledges* one and cannot create one.
- **The tag paths.** `[PLC]` is the only home for a tag path string; `FS-04` owns the push
  mechanism.
- **Where the shared writes land** — [`FS-15`](FS-15-shared-schema-boundary.md).
- **FL2's spool check-in** — [`FS-11`](FS-11-spool-lifecycle-fl2.md).
- **Rod receiving and planning**, which are upstream systems (`[E2E]` stages 1–2).

**Twenty-three register items — by far the most of any category — and they need reading in groups.**

**The transaction has no recovery path.** ⛔ **`OI-39`** is the most serious item here:
*cross-database check-in has no defined recovery path*. **`G2`** states the span — `FlatWireDB`'s
run, check-in and SPC rows plus the shared `reqsum` and `wip_coil_orders`. The database half is one
ACID transaction; **the PLC half is compensation, not rollback**. So a failure after the tag push
leaves the controller configured for a run the database does not have, and nothing says what to do.

**The weld feature is half-homed.** **`G26`** — the merged weld write straddles two phases;
**`G27`** — the weld screen's rod queue and traceability chain have no host; **`OI-59`** — weld
attribution and limits, plus the rework-weld certificate question, are TBD. Together they mean weld
capture is specified across a retired screen and two live ones.

**`ITInhibit` is decided and unmatched.** **`G80`** — the client's **eight** IT inhibit reasons and
`[PLC §8.2]`'s **five** set conditions do not correspond. **`G81`** — *"No Qualified Operators Are
Logged In"* presumes a mechanism that does not exist. **`PLC-Q12`** — the format and lifetime of the
material-tracking identifier `ITInhibit` depends on is unconfirmed.

**Three closed blockers are still cited.** ~~`G19`~~, ~~`G21`~~ and ~~`G22`~~ are resolved and
remain in `blocked_by`. On the board they read as live.

**Eight retired prefixes** — `OQ-3`, `OQ-6`, `OQ-14`, `OQ-22`, `OQ-23`, `OQ-24`, `OQ-25`, `OQ-73` —
resolve to no register (`G61`). **This category alone holds eight of the twenty-one.**

**Gaps this category owns that no story cites:**

- ⛔ **`OI-39` has no owning story.** The module's hardest transaction has no defined recovery
  path and nothing scheduled to define one.
- **`G17`** — `rod`→`coils` multiplies cross-database logical FKs on every `Rod.Alpha` reference,
  and nothing enforces them.
- **`OI-108`** / **`OI-109`** — two unresolved UX questions: whether *Welds this run* is absent or
  present-and-unavailable at cold start, and where the operator lands after *Acknowledge & Begin
  Check-In*.

## 8. Change-impact profile

**What a change here affects.** More than any other operator-facing category, because it is the
entry point.

| A change to… | Affects |
|---|---|
| **the check-in transaction** | `FS-15`'s shared writes, `FS-04`'s tag push, and every category downstream of an open run. `OI-39`'s missing recovery path is the highest-value gap to close in the module |
| **the acknowledgement** | `FS-05`'s consumer contract and `FS-04`'s payload. If `Q48` allows two pass schedules on one rod, this flow changes shape |
| **`ITInhibit`** | `FS-04`'s built `ITInhibitService`, and `FS-18`'s `ItInhibitReason` lookup that `FW-257` re-points at |
| **weld capture** | `FS-12`'s `CoilTraceability` genealogy and `FS-16`'s contractual `NFR012`. A field not captured here cannot be certified there |
| **`RodStaging`** | `FS-10`'s carry-forward, which re-enters through pre-check-in |

**Existing implementation to modify.** Two activities are `in-review` — `FW-157`'s
`CheckInService` and `FW-159`'s write path — and four are `blocked`. So **the check-in service and
its write path are being reviewed while `OI-39` leaves their failure behaviour undefined**. That is
the most consequential instance of the pattern seen in `FS-10`, `FS-11` and `FS-12`: the mechanism
settles before the decision it depends on.

**Regression areas.**

- **The PLC half cannot be rolled back.** Any change to the transaction has to answer what happens
  after a successful push and a failed commit, and today nothing does.
- **`FW-N24` moves three writes from pre-check-in to check-in**, so anything assuming the old
  placement is stale — including `FS-15`'s procedures.
- **Pre-check-in is `Should`.** Descoping it would strand `FS-10`'s carry-forward, which re-enters
  through it, and `FW-063`'s weld dialog, which lives on DB2A.
- **Three closed and eight phantom blockers** make this category's real status hard to read, and a
  planner trusting the count will mis-sequence it.
